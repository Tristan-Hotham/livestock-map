Where the animals are

Cattle on the pampas, sheep on Welsh hills, pigs in the southern Netherlands, buffaloes along the Indus and {{world.sp.chickens.n|bn}} chickens nearly everywhere. A map of the world's farm animals every 10 km, and the story of how the herds and flocks have changed since {{meta.first|raw}}.

Farm animals are so familiar that it is easy to forget how many there are. There are about {{world.sp.cattle.n|bn}} cattle, {{world.sp.sheep.n|bn}} sheep, {{world.sp.goats.n|bn}} goats and {{world.sp.pigs.n|bn}} pigs, and around {{world.sp.chickens.n|bn}} chickens, all alive at the same moment. But where are they? Not where the people are, and not spread evenly over the grass. Some countries are a single animal's country; some valleys hold more pigs than any city holds people.

This page puts them on a map. The FAO's Gridded Livestock of the World estimates how many cattle, buffaloes, sheep, goats, pigs, chickens, ducks and horses lived in every 10 km square in 2015, by spreading census counts across the land with a statistical model. FAOSTAT gives each country's herds and flocks every year from {{meta.first|raw}} to {{meta.last|raw}}, for eighteen kinds of animal from camels to guinea pigs. Pick an animal, then tap anywhere on the map.

    03 · Since {{meta.first|raw}}The chicken century

    Since {{meta.first|raw}} the world's human population has grown {{world.pop_x|f1}} times. Chickens have grown {{world.sp.chickens.x|f1}} times, from {{world.sp.chickens.n0|bn}} to {{world.sp.chickens.n|bn}}, and ducks {{world.sp.ducks.x|f1}} times. Goats ({{world.sp.goats.x|f1}} times) and camels ({{world.sp.camels.x|f1}}) have grown faster than people, mostly in Africa and Asia, which now hold {{goats_afr_asia_share|pc}} of the world's goats. Cattle have grown more slowly than people: there were {{world.cattle_pp0|f2}} cattle per person in {{meta.first|raw}} and {{world.cattle_pp|f2}} in {{meta.last|raw}}. Horses and mules have fallen, as tractors and lorries took their work.

    04 · OutnumberedWhere animals outnumber people

    In {{mam_pp.n_more|int}} of the {{mam_pp.n_countries|int}} countries with at least a million people, farm mammals outnumber humans. Mongolia is the extreme: {{mongolia.mammals|m}} sheep, goats, cattle, horses and camels for {{mongolia.pop|m}} people, {{mongolia.pp|f1}} each, and the most goats ({{pp.goats.top.0.pp|f1}}) and sheep ({{pp.sheep.top.0.pp|f1}}) per person anywhere. Uruguay has {{pp.cattle.top.0.pp|f1}} cattle per person, the most in the world, and Denmark is the only country of a million people or more with more pigs than people ({{pp.pigs.top.0.pp|f1}} each).

    New Zealand is the country of sheep jokes, and the joke is getting old. Its flock peaked in {{nz.peak_year|raw}} at {{nz.peak|m}}, {{nz.peak_pp|f1}} sheep per person. By {{meta.last|raw}} it was down {{nz.fall_pct|pc}} to {{nz.last|m}}, {{nz.last_pp|f1}} per person, while its cattle herd grew by about a fifth. Australia has {{pp.sheep.top.2.pp|f1}} sheep per person, and the Sahel countries, from Mauritania and Mali to Chad and South Sudan, all appear among the countries where sheep, goats or cattle outnumber people.

    05 · ConcentrationOne country's pigs, another's buffaloes

    Some animals are spread around the world; others belong mostly to one country. China has {{conc.pigs.top1_pct|pc}} of the world's pigs, {{conc.ducks.top1_pct|pc}} of its ducks and {{conc.geese.top1_pct|pc}} of its geese. India has {{conc.buffalo.top1_pct|pc}} of the buffaloes, and India, Pakistan and China together have {{conc.buffalo.top3_pct|pc}}. A quarter of the world's camels ({{conc.camels.top1_pct|pc}}) are in Chad. Cattle, sheep and horses are shared more widely: the biggest herds, in Brazil, China and Mexico, hold {{conc.cattle.top1_pct|pc}}, {{conc.sheep.top1_pct|pc}} and {{conc.horses.top1_pct|pc}}.

    06 · The main animalA world of cattle, with islands of pigs and sheep

    To compare a cow with a chicken, farm statistics use livestock units, a rough measure of size and feed: in FAOSTAT's units a cow counts for about {{grid.coef.cattle|f2}} of a unit on world average, a sheep or goat for about {{grid.coef.sheep|f2}}, a pig {{grid.coef.pigs|f2}} and a chicken {{grid.coef.chickens|f2}}. Weighted this way, cattle are the main farm animal in {{main.counts.cattle|int}} of {{main.n|int}} countries, chickens in {{main.counts.chickens|int}}, pigs in {{main.counts.pigs|int}} and sheep in {{main.counts.sheep|int}}. Across all countries cattle make up {{lsu_world.share.cattle|pc}} of the world's livestock units.

    The "Main animal" layer of the map does the same for every 10 km square. Cattle lead on {{grid.dom_pct.cattle|pc}} of the world's land. The exceptions make a map of their own: pigs across China's farm belt and in Denmark, the southern Netherlands and Catalonia, buffaloes along the Ganges and Indus, sheep in Patagonia, the Karoo, western and south-eastern Australia and the hills of Wales, sheep and goats along the desert edges of the Sahel, Arabia, Somalia and Iran, and chickens in the American south-east, Arabia and Iran. About {{grid.empty_pct|pc}} of the land has less than one livestock unit per square kilometre.

    07 · Britain and IrelandSheep hills, dairy pasture and poultry plains

    The United Kingdom has {{uk.fao.sheep|m}} sheep, more than any other country in Europe outside Turkey, against {{uk.fao.cattle|m}} cattle, {{uk.fao.pigs|m}} pigs and {{uk.fao.chickens|m0}} chickens. By livestock units cattle are its main animal ({{uk.main_share|pc}}), then sheep ({{uk.lsu_share.sheep|pc}}). The grid shows the country divided by its hills. Wales has {{uk.wales_area_share|pc}} of the UK's land but {{uk.nations.Wales.sheep_share|pc}} of its sheep, {{uk.wales_sheep_x_england|f1}} times England's density, and Powys alone has {{uk.top_sheep_n.0.share|pc}} of all UK sheep. Cattle are densest in Northern Ireland ({{uk.nations.Northern Ireland.cattle_km2|int}} per km²) and the dairy pastures of Cheshire, Cornwall and Somerset. Pigs and chickens belong to the east: {{uk.nations.England.pigs_share|pc}} of UK pigs are in England, the most in North Yorkshire, Norfolk and Suffolk and the densest in the East Riding of Yorkshire, and Herefordshire and Lincolnshire have the most chickens per square kilometre.

    08 · How I did thisMethods and caveats

    The grid. Gridded Livestock of the World version 4 (GLW 4) gives the number of cattle, buffaloes, sheep, goats, pigs, chickens, ducks and horses in each 5 arc minute cell (about 10 km at the equator) in 2015. FAO and its partners collected sub-national census counts, fitted random forest models of animal density against climate, land cover, access to markets and other covariates, and used the predictions to share each census total among the cells inside it (the 'dasymetric' layer used here). Summed over the world, the grid matches FAOSTAT's 2015 totals to within a few per cent ({{grid.vs_fao2015.cattle|f1}}% for cattle). It is a model: it is most reliable where census areas are small, and it can spread animals thinly over remote land or pile them near towns where the covariates say so.

    Counts by country. FAOSTAT's 'Stocks' element, the number of animals alive at one time in each year, {{meta.first|raw}} to {{meta.last|raw}}, matched to Natural Earth countries. China is mainland China (FAOSTAT also has a 'China' total that adds Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao). Former states (the USSR, Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia and others) count in world totals but have no country of their own here, so country series for their successors start in the early 1990s. Per person figures use World Bank population for the same year.

    Livestock units. FAOSTAT's livestock patterns tables give each country's stocks in livestock units; dividing by head gives each country's own factor for each animal (ducks take the chicken factor). The main animal of a country or a 10 km square is the one with most livestock units; squares with less than one unit per km² are left blank. UK county figures share each 10 km square among the counties it touches by area.

    What this is not. These are animals alive at one moment, not animals slaughtered in a year: a broiler chicken lives about six weeks, so far more chickens are raised each year than are alive at once. FAOSTAT figures are official national figures and FAO estimates, of uneven quality; nomadic herds are hard to count. The grid is for 2015 and does not show change since. Wild animals, reindeer, fish and insects are not included.
